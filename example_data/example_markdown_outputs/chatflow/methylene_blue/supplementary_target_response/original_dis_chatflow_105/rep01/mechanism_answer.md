### Literature-Supported Typical Mechanisms
Literature regarding hydrophilic matrix systems consistently establishes that the viscosity grade and ratio of HPMC are critical determinants of release kinetics. High-viscosity grades (like HPMC K4M or K15M) possess longer polymer chains that entangle more effectively upon hydration, forming a thicker, more viscous, and mechanically stronger gel layer that significantly retards drug diffusion and protects the core from rapid erosion. Conversely, low-viscosity grades (like HPMC 100LV) hydrate rapidly but form weaker gel structures with lower viscosity, leading to faster chain disentanglement, increased surface erosion, and consequently, a faster drug release rate. In pH-switching environments, the ionization state of the drug (methylene blue is cationic) and the potential pH-dependent solubility can interact with the gel layer; however, HPMC itself is largely non-ionic and less sensitive to pH changes compared to other polymers, meaning the release profile is primarily governed by the diffusional path length and gel strength established during the initial acidic phase and maintained in the neutral phase. The flow rate of 1 mL/min in a flow-through cell provides a constant sink condition that emphasizes the erosion-diffusion balance, where insufficient gel strength leads to rapid washout of polymer fragments, while optimal gel strength sustains a zero-order or near-zero-order release via Fickian diffusion through the gel layer.
