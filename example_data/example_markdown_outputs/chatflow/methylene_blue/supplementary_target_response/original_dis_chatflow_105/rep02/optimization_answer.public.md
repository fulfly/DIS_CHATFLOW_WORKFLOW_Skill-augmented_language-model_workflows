### Phenomenon Review
During the 6.25 to 11.25-hour window, the high-viscosity Methylene Blue tablet exhibits a stable, cohesive hydrogel state characterized by a deep blue core surrounded by a thick, rough, and "fuzzy" gel layer, indicating significant hydration without structural collapse. The tablet maintains its overall spherical/ovoid outline with only slight surface irregularities, suggesting that the rate of polymer swelling is balanced by a slow, surface-limited erosion process rather than catastrophic disintegration. While the surrounding medium shows a consistent light blue tint from dissolved dye, a distinct sediment layer of fine eroded fragments has accumulated at the bottom of the cell, implying that the eroded matrix particles are dense enough to settle quickly rather than remaining suspended. This behavior highlights a sustained-release mechanism where the high-viscosity HPMC matrix effectively retards rapid breakup, maintaining integrity while gradually shedding material.

### Literature-Supported Typical Mechanisms
Existing literature establishes that Hydroxypropyl Methylcellulose (HPMC) functions as a hydrophilic polysaccharide that undergoes simultaneous swelling and dissolution upon contact with aqueous media. The release mechanism is governed by two concurrent processes: drug diffusion through the hydrated gel layer and matrix erosion as the outer gel layer dissolves or sloughs off. Crucially, the molecular weight (viscosity grade) of HPMC is a dominant factor; higher viscosity grades (such as the K4M/100lv ratios likely used here) form thicker, more robust gel layers with higher mechanical strength compared to lower viscosity grades. This enhanced gel strength delays the erosion front, shifting the release kinetics toward a more erosion-controlled or mixed diffusion-erosion profile, thereby preventing the rapid burst release often seen in lower viscosity matrices. The formation of a coherent gel barrier is essential for sustaining release over extended periods (e.g., 12–24 hours), as it regulates the diffusion path length for the drug and protects the inner core from immediate solvent penetration.

### Tentative Mechanistic Interpretation for the Current Product
Based on the observed phenomena and literature principles, the following chain of reasoning is proposed as a hypothesis:
*   **Observed Phenomena:** The preservation of the tablet's macroscopic shape despite a highly granular, eroding surface, combined with the accumulation of settled sediment, suggests a "swelling-erosion equilibrium."
*   **Possible Formulation/Process Design Factors:** The use of a **high-viscosity HPMC formulation** is the primary driver. The long polymer chains in high-viscosity grades entangle more effectively upon hydration, creating a gel layer with sufficient viscoelasticity to resist the shear forces of the flow-through cell (1 mL/min). This prevents the "catastrophic breakup" typical of low-viscosity matrices. The pH switch from 1.2 to 6.8 appears to have minimal disruptive effect on the gel integrity in this specific formulation, suggesting the HPMC matrix is robust across this pH range.
*   **Possible Release Behaviour:** The release is likely controlled by a combination of **diffusion through the thick gel layer** (explaining the steady blue tint in the medium) and **surface erosion** (explaining the sediment buildup). The "fuzzy" texture indicates that the erosion front is active but slow; the gel layer sheds small, dense fragments that settle rather than dispersing widely. Consequently, the drug release profile is expected to be linear or near-zero-order during this window, avoiding the rapid decline associated with matrix collapse.
*   *Caveat:* These interpretations remain hypotheses. Without direct measurement of the gel layer thickness, rheological properties of the swollen matrix, or precise drug concentration profiles over time, we cannot definitively quantify the contribution of diffusion versus erosion.

Given that the current evidence relies primarily on visual observation and general literature principles, the following experiments are recommended to validate the mechanistic hypotheses:
1.  **Comparative Dissolution Studies:** Conduct parallel dissolution tests using Low-viscosity and Standard-viscosity formulations under identical conditions. 2.  **Gel Layer Characterization:** Utilize techniques such as Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI) or optical coherence tomography to non-invasively measure the thickness and water content profile of the gel layer at the 6–12 hour mark. This would confirm if the "fuzzy" appearance correlates with a specific hydration gradient.
3.  **Agitation Sensitivity Analysis:** Vary the flow rate in the flow-through cell (e.g., increase to 4–8 mL/min) to determine the critical shear stress required to cause catastrophic failure of the gel layer. This would validate the hypothesis that the current flow rate (1 mL/min) is insufficient to overcome the mechanical strength of the high-viscosity gel.
4.  **Sediment Analysis:** Collect and analyze the settled sediment particles for residual drug content. If the sediment contains significant amounts of unreleased drug, it suggests that erosion is removing drug-loaded matrix before full diffusion can occur, which would necessitate a reformulation to optimize the diffusion/erosion ratio.
