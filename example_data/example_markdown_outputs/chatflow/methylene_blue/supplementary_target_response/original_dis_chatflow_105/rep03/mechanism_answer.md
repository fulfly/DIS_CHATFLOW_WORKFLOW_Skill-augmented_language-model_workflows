### Literature-Supported Typical Mechanisms
Existing literature on hydrophilic matrices confirms that Hydroxypropyl Methylcellulose (HPMC) functions through a dual mechanism involving simultaneous swelling (gel formation) and matrix erosion. As noted in retrieved studies, increasing the proportion of HPMC (e.g., up to 15%) leads to the formation of a thicker, more viscous gel layer, which significantly slows diffusion rates and can prevent the drug from releasing in a steady state, sometimes resulting in incomplete release within standard testing windows. The balance between diffusion and erosion is critical; while lower viscosity grades or lower polymer loads typically favor faster erosion and potential fragmentation, higher viscosity ratios promote a durable gel barrier that restricts drug movement to slow diffusion. Consequently, formulations with optimized high-viscosity HPMC ratios are known to maintain structural integrity for extended periods, effectively delaying the onset of significant erosion until the very late stages of dissolution.
