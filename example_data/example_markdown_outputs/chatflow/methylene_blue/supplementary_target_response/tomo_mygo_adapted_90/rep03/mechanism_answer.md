# Literature-Supported Typical Mechanistic Links

**HPMC Matrix Mechanisms:**
Literature on hydrophilic matrix tablets establishes that release is governed by two primary, often competing, mechanisms:
1.  **Diffusion:** Drug molecules diffuse through the hydrated gel layer formed by the polymer.
2.  **Erosion:** The outer gel layer erodes or dissolves, exposing fresh drug-polymer matrix.

**Impact of Polymer Viscosity and Ratio:**
*   **High-Viscosity HPMC (e.g., K4M):** Forms a thicker, more viscous, and cohesive gel layer upon hydration. This gel layer acts as a stronger barrier to diffusion and resists erosion, leading to slower, more sustained release profiles. It is particularly effective in maintaining matrix integrity under agitation (flow-through conditions).
*   **Low-Viscosity HPMC (e.g., 100lv):** Hydrates rapidly but forms a weaker, less viscous gel. This facilitates faster water penetration into the matrix core and allows for easier erosion or disintegration of the gel layer. Consequently, formulations with higher proportions of low-viscosity HPMC typically exhibit faster initial release rates and potentially shorter overall release durations.

**pH Dependence:**
HPMC is non-ionic and generally pH-independent in its swelling behavior. However, the solubility of the drug (Methylene Blue) and the erosion rate of the matrix can be influenced by the medium's ionic strength and pH. The shift from pH 1.2 to 6.8 at 1 hour may alter the hydration dynamics slightly, but the primary driver of the observed differences among the three formulations is attributed to the polymer blend ratios rather than pH-specific polymer ionization.

**Gel Layer Integrity:**
The ratio of high-to-low viscosity polymers determines the "strength" of the gel barrier. A higher proportion of K4M (high viscosity) reinforces the gel, reducing the likelihood of premature rupture or rapid erosion. Conversely, a higher proportion of 100lv (low viscosity) weakens the gel structure, promoting faster breakdown and quicker drug access to the dissolution medium.
